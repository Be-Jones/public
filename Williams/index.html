How to use this page

  • Select a section heading to open or close it.
  • Select Copy on any prompt and paste it into Copilot. To try one on your own work afterward, swap in your own project, people, meetings, and files.

  • Copilot only uses content you already have permission to open, and nothing is sent, moved, or changed until you confirm it.

Weekly leadership update, every MondayNew

Set this up as a weekly scheduled task that runs every Monday at 7:00 a.m. my time.

Reconstruct what I accomplished at work over the past 7 days and email me a concise leadership update to use in my next manager check-in.

Sources to review for the last 7 days: emails I sent and threads I actively drove (Sent Items, read the full message for substantive replies); Teams chats and channel messages where I contributed meaningfully (read my own messages plus enough surrounding context to understand them); meetings I led, organized, presented in, or owned follow-up for; and files I created or edited (Word, PowerPoint, Excel, Loop, OneNote). Check the content of key decks or documents I authored so they can be described accurately. Focus on work that moved something forward. Ignore routine back-and-forth, FYI noise, meeting accept/decline notices, status-only meetings, recurring syncs that produced no decision, deliverable or next step, and personal or social chats.

Send one email to me only, subject "Leadership update: <start date> – <end date>", with exactly three sections:

1. Accomplished: what was completed, progressed or became clearer. Be specific and tie each item to a real artifact, email thread, chat or meeting (name the stakeholder, the artifact or thread, and the date). Include nothing that cannot be traced back to a source.
2. Needs input: open questions, blockers, decisions, or areas where leadership input or help would be useful. If there is nothing substantive, write "n/a."
3. Looking ahead: work continuing or starting in the coming week, inferred from this week's activity and the upcoming calendar. Do not transcribe the calendar. Name a specific meeting or session only if it is real work I am driving, and skip routine or recurring syncs.

Keep the email skimmable in under one minute: short bullets, bolded customer or topic leads, no padding. If something might not belong, or its attribution is unclear (for example, which customer a chat refers to), mark it "(uncertain)" inline instead of guessing. If a section is thin, add a short final line, "Context to add:", saying what I should add rather than padding the section. If there was almost no meaningful activity this week (for example, I was out of office), still send a brief email that says so and lists any open items.
