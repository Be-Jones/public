The demos, step by step

1 Warm-up and personalization 0:00-0:04 | Copilot app

What you'll see: The same question can get different answers. Personalization teaches Copilot how you work, so its answers fit you better.

Warm-up: everyone try this

  1. Open Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat in the Copilot app, Teams or your browser.
  2. Run the prompt below, then compare your answer with the person next to you.
Warm-up prompt
What are the TEAM, Inc. brand colors?

Personalization

  1. In the Copilot app, go to Settings > Personalization.
  2. Under custom instructions, start from one of the built-in samples and apply it.
  3. Under saved memories, see what Copilot remembers, then add a memory with the prompt below.
  4. Ask the follow-up to see the memory in use.
Prompt
Remember that I support inspection and maintenance planning for our refining and pipeline operations, and that I like a short summary table before the details.
Follow-up
What do you know about how I like to work?
Answers differ because Copilot writes each response fresh and may draw on different web sources. A clear prompt plus personalization makes answers more consistent and more useful.
2 Chat builds the prompt, Researcher runs it 0:04-0:09 | Copilot Chat

What you'll see: Use Chat to think through what you need, then hand the deep research to Researcher in the same conversation.

  1. In Copilot Chat, describe what you need and ask Copilot to help write a Researcher prompt.
  2. Copilot asks clarifying questions. Answer them all in one reply.
  3. Copilot returns a refined prompt. Type @Researcher in the same chat and paste it in.
  4. Researcher works for several minutes and returns a report with citations. We'll come back to it in Word.
Chat prompt
I need to brief our operations leaders on how robotics, drones and AI are changing inspection work at refineries, gas plants and pipelines. I want to learn about the technologies already being used and what's being tested, where they're being used, and what the risks/benefits/ROI looks like. Help me write a strong prompt for Researcher. Ask any questions you need to help write a good prompt
Answers to Copilot's questions
Audience: operations leaders for refining, gas processing and pipelines.
Purpose: a briefing to help them decide which technologies to look at next.
Technologies: drones, crawlers and other inspection robots, permanently installed sensors, and AI that analyzes inspection data.
Geography and time frame: U.S. first, with notable international examples, from the last three years.
ROI: use published figures where they exist (cost, downtime, inspection time, safety exposure) and say when a number is a vendor claim or an estimate.
Sources: public industry, regulator and operator sources.
Output: executive summary, a comparison table by technology, and cited sources.
Example Researcher prompt
@Researcher Research how robotics, drones and AI are changing inspection work at refineries, gas processing plants and pipelines, for a briefing to operations leaders. Cover: (1) the technologies already in production use, such as aerial and confined-space drones, magnetic crawlers, in-line inspection robots, permanently installed sensors and AI for defect detection; (2) what is being tested or piloted now and how close it is to routine use; (3) where each is being used, by facility type and inspection task, with named operator examples; (4) the benefits, risks and ROI, using published figures for cost, downtime, inspection time and safety exposure, and noting which figures come from vendors; and (5) the main barriers to adoption, such as regulatory acceptance, data quality, workforce skills and cybersecurity. Focus on the U.S., with notable international examples, from the last three years. Write it with an executive summary, a comparison table by technology and cited sources.
Researcher cites each claim, so you can check what it relied on before you share the report.
When you call Researcher with @ inside a chat, it picks its model automatically. Open Researcher on its own if you want to choose.

4 Inbox, calendar and drafting 0:13-0:19 | Outlook

What you'll see: Copilot catches you up, books the time, moves what can move and writes in your voice.

a. Catch up

  1. Open a long email and read the summary Copilot puts at the top.
  2. Open Copilot in Outlook and ask for a catch-up across related emails.
Prompt
Summarize my emails about RB-C-201 and list what each person needs from me and by when.

b. Schedule, and let Copilot move a conflict

  1. Ask Copilot to schedule a meeting. This one overlaps an existing 1:1.
  2. Send the invite. Because the 1:1 allows it, Copilot moves the 1:1 to an open time and lets you know in the notifications pane.
Prompt
Schedule a 30-minute Teams meeting with Karin Blair and Jamie Reding this Friday at 10:00 a.m. to review the RB-C-201 corrective inspection findings.

c. Draft Instructions, then draft

  1. In Outlook, open the Copilot menu > Settings > Draft Instructions and turn on Use custom instructions when drafting email. Add your instructions and save.
  2. Reply to an email with Draft with Copilot. The draft follows your instructions on length, layout and sign-off.
Draft Instructions
Keep emails under 120 words. Lead with the answer or decision in the first sentence. Use bullets for actions, each with an owner and a date. Use American English and a plain, friendly tone. Sign off with my first name.
Drafting prompt
Reply to Laurence confirming I'll cover RB-C-201 at the Midstream Leadership Review. Summarize what happened, the cost and what is still open, using the latest emails from the team.
To let Copilot move a meeting, open it and turn on If conflicts arise, let Copilot reschedule this event, then pick acceptable times. It works for personal appointments and 1:1s, and attendees just see a normal update.
The move can take a minute or two to show up.
5 Edit the Researcher report 0:19-0:23 | Word

What you'll see: Copilot edits like a careful colleague: every change visible and reversible.

  1. Export the Researcher report to Word.
  2. Turn on Track Changes (Review > Track Changes).
  3. Look at the reference list and pick a source you don't want to rely on.
  4. Ask Copilot to remove it, then review each redline and accept or reject it.
Prompt
Remove every statement and citation that relies on [source name]. Leave everything else unchanged, and make each edit a tracked change.
A skill is a saved, repeatable way of working that Copilot can call on. You'll see a built-in legal skill next, two in PowerPoint, and in Excel you'll build your own.
6 Contract review against a playbook 0:23-0:27 | Word desktop

What you'll see: A first-pass legal review in minutes, grounded in your own standards, with every suggested change tracked.

  1. Open the agreement in Word: Zava_MSA_Proposed_Meridian.docx.
  2. In the Copilot pane, choose the legal review skill and point it to the company playbook: ZavaEnergy_MSA_Contract_Standards_Playbook.docx.
  3. Review the clause-by-clause table, then let Copilot propose tracked edits.
Prompt
Review this agreement against the MSA Contract Standards Playbook (LEG-MSA-STD-2026). Give me a table with each clause, the playbook standard, pass or deviation, and the approval it needs. Mark standards this draft doesn't cover as 'not addressed' rather than a deviation. Then propose tracked edits for the strict-standard deviations.
This is a first pass against your own standards, with each finding tied to a clause. A lawyer still makes the call.

8 Interactive slide 0:31-0:33 | PowerPoint

What you'll see: A slide your audience can click through, from a single prompt.

10 Clean, analyze, chart, save as a skill 0:35-0:42 | Excel

What you'll see: Messy exports are normal. Copilot spots the problems, fixes them with you, and turns the routine into a reusable skill.

  1. Open the work-order export: Red Butte Maintenance Work Order Export - October 2026.xlsx, and format the data as a table (Ctrl+T).
  2. Ask a question about the data. Copilot warns you about inconsistent data before it answers.
  3. Add a calculated column.
  4. Run @clean-data, review what it found and apply the fixes.
  5. Build the charts.
  6. Save what you just did as your own skill with @skill-builder, then find it under Add > Choose plugins and skills.
Data quality warning
What were our biggest maintenance costs at Red Butte in October, by equipment?
Calculated column
Add a column called Status Group that labels each work order Completed or Open based on STATUS.
Clean data
@clean-data Find and fix the data issues in this table. Show me what you found and why it matters before you change anything.
Charts
Create a new sheet with 2 graphs: one should be a vertically stacked bar chart with each bar representing a crew, and each bar broken down into 3 sections: where actual total cost was less than estimated cost, where it was equal to estimated cost, and where actual cost exceeded estimated cost. The other graph should show a breakdown in a pie chart by worktype. Don't show data labels where the value is 0.
Save as a skill
@skill-builder Turn what I just did to create these graphs into a custom skill named "work-order-graphs"
Skills you create can show up in other apps too, including Chat and PowerPoint, and you can turn each one on or off.
11 Deeper analysis with Analyst 0:42-0:45 | Copilot app

What you'll see: Analyst works like a data scientist: it writes and runs Python to clean the data, answer harder questions and show its work.

  1. In the Copilot app, open the Analyst agent.
  2. Attach the same work-order export, in its original messy form: Red Butte Maintenance Work Order Export - October 2026.xlsx.
  3. Send the prompt. Analyst reasons through the problem step by step, writing and running code as it goes.
  4. Open the code it ran, then review the answer, the chart and the takeaways.
Analyst prompt
@Analyst Using the attached Red Butte work-order export, clean the data first and tell me what you fixed. Then, for completed work orders, show which crews and work types go over their estimated cost most often, with the total and average overrun for each, and list the open work orders that were past their target date as of November 10, 2026. Finish with a chart and three takeaways for the maintenance manager.
Use Copilot in Excel to work inside your spreadsheet. Use Analyst when the question needs several steps of reasoning or analysis across messy or multiple files.
You can download the chart, or the cleaned data Analyst produced, from its response.

Writing a good prompt

Strong prompts usually cover four things. You don't need all four every time, but the more you give, the better the answer.

PartWhat to includeExample
GoalWhat you want backDraft a one-page pre-job briefing
ContextWhy you need it and who it's forfor a heat treating crew starting at a refinery next week
SourcesWhich files, emails, meetings or sites to useusing the scope document and last week's kickoff meeting
ExpectationsFormat, length and toneas a checklist with hazards, PPE and hold points
  • Iterate: treat the first answer as a draft and ask for changes.
  • Ask Copilot to ask you questions first when you aren't sure what it needs.
  • Check what matters: open the citations before you share anything.

Common questions

QuestionShort answer
Is our data used to train the model?No. Copilot and Researcher work with the content you already have access to, and your organization's data isn't used to train the models.
Can we stop Researcher from using certain websites?Not site by site. Admins can turn web search on or off for the organization.
Will the other person know Copilot moved our meeting?They get a normal meeting update, the same as if you'd moved it yourself.
Where do the skills I create live?In a skills folder in your OneDrive. You can turn each one on or off, and edit or remove it.
Is the contract review legal advice?No. It's a first pass against your own playbook, with each finding tied to a clause. A lawyer still decides.
What's the difference between Analyst and Copilot in Excel?Copilot in Excel works inside your open workbook. Analyst is an agent in the Copilot app that reasons in several steps and runs Python, so it suits messier data, multiple files and deeper questions.
Do people need a license to use an interactive slide?No. Anyone you share the deck with can click through it.
Who can create a company Brand Kit?Brand managers with the right permissions. Official kits are managed centrally; personal kits are for individual use.
